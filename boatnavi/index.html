Every chart app shows depth.
BoatNavi shows clearance.

Depth is the water. Water level is the tide. Neither is the number you need. BoatNavi does the arithmetic for your boat, colours the whole chart with the answer, and lets you drag the clock to see it change.

clearance=charted depth+water level−your draft−your margin

One Screen, One Question

A chart where the water is coloured by clearance for your boat, and a scrubber under it. Drag the scrubber, the whole map recolours. Red means you go aground.

🗺️

The Chart

Clearance-coloured water with a 48-hour scrubber that snaps to high and low water. Tap anywhere for the depth, the water level, the clearance, the survey source and the survey year.

📉

Track Profile

Depth against distance along your route, with the water line at the scrubbed time. The controlling depth is called out explicitly, and any hazard within 150 ft of the track is named.

🕓

Window Finder

When can I go? Sweeps the next 48 hours and returns the passable windows — evaluating the controlling point at your arrival there, not at departure.

⚠️

Hazards

Wrecks, obstructions, rocks, pilings and foul ground from the ENC, in three classes. A hazard with a known depth reddens the water through the ordinary formula; one without is a hard exclusion the solver refuses to route through.

📡

Position

Your boat's NMEA gateway over its own Wi‑Fi, the phone's GNSS, or an external receiver. Always draws the accuracy circle, and degrades the headline number to a range when the fix can't support a single one.

🔁

Sounder Loop

Predicted depth beside measured depth from your own transducer. Divergence says so loudly, and every comparison is logged into a personal correction layer for next time.

📍

Trips & Marks

Record where you actually went with the depth you actually saw, reuse it as a known-good route, and drop labelled waypoints or private hazards from the cockpit.

✏️

Built for iPad Too

Chart and profile side by side on one scrubber, routes drawn with Apple Pencil, and keyboard shortcuts for the nav station.

From Dock to Answer

Set it up once. After that, the only input is your finger on the scrubber.

1

Tell It About Your Boat

Your draft and the margin you're comfortable with. That's the whole profile. Everything on the chart is computed for that boat, not a generic one.

2

Download Your Regions

Chart packs carry the soundings, the depth areas, the hazards, and the tide constituents for every station in the region. Fetch them at the dock, before you leave the last bar of signal behind.

3

Drag the Clock, Read the Colour

The scrubber runs 48 hours ahead. Every position on the chart recolours as the tide moves — green is water you can use, red is water you can't.

4

Ask When You Can Go

Draw the route, and the window finder tells you which departure times get you through the shallow bit with clearance to spare — timed for when you'd actually reach it.

Does it need a signal? No.

Offline is the operating assumption, not a feature — there is no cell service in most of the Delta. Exactly one thing needs a network, and the app tells you when it's missing.

WhatNeeds a Network?
Charted depthNo — it's in the downloaded pack
Tide predictionNo — computed on the device from harmonic constituents cached in the pack, indefinitely
Your positionNo — GPS is satellite, or your boat's NMEA gateway over the boat's own Wi‑Fi
Clearance, track profile, window finderNo — all of it runs on the two above
Live tide correction (observed level, SFBOFS model)Yes — and this is the only one

When the live correction is missing, the chart falls back to the harmonic prediction and says so, with a persistent “predicted tide only — no live correction” chip. It never degrades silently.

Measured, Not Promised

The tide engine is checked against NOAA's own published predictions before anything else is built on it.

A Planning Aid, Not a Navigation System

Public bathymetry outside dredged channels is old — some of it dates to 2004 — and Delta shoals move. The SFBOFS model is guidance, not measurement. Proximity alerts are advisory and are not a replacement for your boat's own depth alarm.

The right mental model: BoatNavi tells you which windows are worth attempting. The depth sounder tells you what is actually under the keel.

Until the app ships, the bundled chart data is synthetic and is labelled SAMPLE — NOT FOR NAVIGATION everywhere it appears. The tide is real; the depths are invented.
